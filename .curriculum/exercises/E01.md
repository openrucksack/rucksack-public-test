# E01 · Pick a job worth doing

level 1 · area: Choosing · about 15 minutes · access: any chat app, a phone is fine

## The idea
Using AI is not automatically progress. Before you start, decide what a good result looks
like, and whether AI is likely to get you there faster than doing it yourself.

## Why it matters to you
Most disappointment with AI comes from starting without a picture of success. Once you
have one, you can tell straight away whether the answer is any good.

## Do it
1. Pick one real, low-risk task you have this week: an email you keep putting off, a
   list, a plan, a short piece of writing. Nothing with other people's private details.
2. Write three success criteria in plain words. "Short, polite, I would actually send it."
3. Do a quick manual version first, even a rough one, and note roughly how long it took.
4. Now do it with AI. Note the time, including any fixing afterwards.
5. Check the AI version against your three criteria.

## What goes in your rucksack
- The result, saved where you will find it (a plain file in the rucksack, or
  `projects/<name>.md` if it is ongoing).
- A line under `## Remember` in today's `memory/` note: the task, whether AI was worth it,
  and roughly how much time it saved or cost.

## The check
You can point to each of your three criteria and say whether the result meets it, and
whether using AI saved time. If doing it yourself was quicker or better, that is a useful
thing to know, and it still counts.

## For the AI running this
If they already did this while setting up, do not repeat it.
Evidence for Choosing level 1. If the person cannot think of a task, offer three from what
they told you in `USER.md`, never a made-up one.
