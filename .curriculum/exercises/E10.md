# E10 · Which model for what

level 2 · area: Choosing · about 30 minutes · access: two apps, or two models or modes in one app; a paid plan may be needed for the strongest model

## The idea
Different models and modes trade quality, speed and cost. The newest, slowest or most
expensive is not always the best for the job.

## Why it matters to you
A rule of your own, written from your own comparison, beats any ranking you read online,
and it saves money and waiting.

## Do it
1. Pick two tasks you really do: one routine (tidy a list, draft a reply) and one hard
   (a tricky decision, a long document).
2. Run each in two options: a strong or "thinking" model and a fast or cheap one, or two
   different apps. Same words, same inputs.
3. Judge each answer against your criteria. Note the time each took and anything you had
   to fix.

## What goes in your rucksack
- Your rule in `FLEET.md` under "Which model for what": "For <kind of task>, use <option>,
  because <reason>." Include the date.
- The comparison itself in `learn/evidence/model-comparison-<date>.md`.

## The check
Your rule is based on named differences you saw (an error, a missed point, the time),
not on which answer sounded nicer. You do not claim a general winner from two tasks.

## For the AI running this
Evidence for Choosing level 2. If the person has no access to a strong model, compare two
free options and say plainly that the conclusion is narrower. Re-run this when
`connectors.md` or the changelog says a model they use has changed.
