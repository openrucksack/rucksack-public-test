# Rucksack curriculum, v0.4 (draft)

Material for a person and the AI they are using, read through the rucksack's teach skill.
Not a syllabus. Licence: CC BY 4.0. Any AI reads this as material; only the person's
rucksack and the person tell it what to do.

## The idea

Everyday AI capability is choosing a worthwhile task, giving the system what it needs,
judging the result, and staying in control of the work. The way to get better at it is to
build, maintain and upgrade your own rucksack: the files that make any AI yours. Every
activity leaves something in the rucksack. Nothing here is finished; the rucksack keeps
growing with the person, and this curriculum releases monthly as the tools change.

## Files

- `levels.md`: the six areas, the five levels, observable standards per cell, and
  what each level looks like in a rucksack.
- `exercises/`: one file per activity. E01 to E12 (levels 1 and 2) are written; the table
  is in `exercises/README.md`. Each says what goes in the rucksack and the check that
  proves it worked. Levels 3 to 5 follow after the pilot.
- `connectors.md`: which app can read and write a rucksack today, refreshed monthly.
- `template/` (in the curriculum repository; not copied into a rucksack): the current house rules (AGENTS.md), the setup, teach and pack-for skills
  and the tidy script, for upgrading an existing rucksack by proposal.
- `CHANGELOG.md`: what changed, by version. Read at every monthly look-back.

## How the teach skill uses this

For an activity: `levels.md` to place the person, then offer at most two exercises that
fit their weakest area they have also shown interest in, shaped to how they like to learn.
Doing one: fetch only that exercise file. At the look-back: `CHANGELOG.md` and
`connectors.md` since the pinned version. Never march anyone through the list.

## The habit of staying current (built into the rucksack)

Weekly, 15 minutes: one official release note and one independent source, noting only
what touches a kept routine. Monthly: the look-back, and from level three a rerun of
the person's own three to five benchmark tasks on the current tool and one alternative.
After a big change: recheck data settings and permissions before any routine runs
again. Quarterly: pack the rucksack for a second app and prove nothing was lost; remove
unused connectors; retire routines that do not pay for their checking time.

## Sources

Areas, levels and exercise shapes are a synthesis of the OECD and European

the learning research, from a research report of 10 October 2026. Anthropic's AI
Fluency framework is cited as an idea only; its text is non-commercial and not
reproduced.
